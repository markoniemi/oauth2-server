This is a wonderful way to help your daughter step onto the property market, but because it involves two different countries (Finland and Sweden), you need to be careful with the paperwork.

In both Finland and Sweden, a loan with **no interest** is perfectly legal and does not trigger automatic gift taxes on the interest you "gave up." However, the tax authorities (**Vero** in Finland and **Skatteverket** in Sweden) look closely at family loans to ensure they aren't hidden gifts.

To prove it is a real loan, you must have a clear, signed promissory note (*velkakirja* in Finnish / *skuldebrev* in Swedish) and a documented bank trail for every payment.

Here is a structured draft of the agreement tailored to your terms.

---

## Intragroup Loan Agreement / Skuldebrev

**1. Parties**

* **Lenders:** [Your Name] and [Wife's Name], residing at [Your Address, Finland].
* **Borrowers:** [Daughter's Name] and [Boyfriend's Name], residing at [Their Address, Sweden].
* *Note: Making both daughter and boyfriend "jointly and severally liable" (solidaarinen vastuu / solidariskt ansvarig) means they are both 100% responsible for the loan. If they break up, you can legally collect from either.*

**2. Loan Amount and Currency**

* The Lenders agree to lend the Borrowers the sum of **[Amount, e.g., €50,000 / 500,000 SEK]**.
* The loan shall be paid out to the Borrowers' designated bank account on or before [Date].
* *Tip: It is highly recommended to issue and repay the loan in a single currency (e.g., Euros) to avoid dispute over currency fluctuations over 10 years.*

**3. Interest (Korko)**

* The loan is entirely **interest-free (0% interest)**.

**4. Repayment Schedule & Flexibility**

* **Term:** The loan has a maximum duration of ten (10) years, with the final balance due no later than [End Date, 2036].
* **Minimum Monthly Payment:** The Borrowers shall pay a minimum of **[e.g., €400 / 4,000 SEK]** per month, due on the [e.g., 30th] day of each calendar month.
* **Payment Flexibility:** The Borrowers are permitted to skip individual monthly payments as necessary, provided that:
1. They notify the Lenders in writing (email/text) prior to the due date.
2. The entire outstanding balance is still fully repaid within the 10-year term.


* **Prepayment:** The Borrowers have the right to repay any amount extra or the entire loan early without penalty.

**5. Default and Acceleration**

* If the Borrowers fail to maintain regular payments without communicating, or if the 10-year term expires with a remaining balance, the Lenders reserve the right to demand full payment of the remaining balance within 90 days.

**6. Governing Law**

* This agreement shall be governed by and construed in accordance with the laws of **Finland** [or Sweden—usually best to pick the country of the Lenders if you want local Finnish enforcement].

**7. Signatures**

* *Must include names, dates, places, and signatures for all four parties.*
* *It is highly recommended to have two independent witnesses sign as well.*

### Three Critical Things to Keep in Mind

1. **The Boyfriend Factor:** If your daughter and her boyfriend split up during these 10 years, things can get messy. Because the money is going toward a flat in Sweden, it may fall under the Swedish Cohabitation Act (*Sambolagen*). Under Swedish law, a shared flat bought for joint use is split 50/50 in a breakup, *regardless of who paid what*. Your daughter and her boyfriend **must** write a separate **Samboavtal** (cohabitation agreement) in Sweden stating that the flat shouldn't be split 50/50, or that your daughter's share reflects the family loan.
2. **The Bank Trail:** Never hand over cash. Transfer the money from your Finnish bank directly to their account, and write "LOAN / VELKA" in the reference field. When they pay you back monthly, they must use the reference "LOAN REPAYMENT / VELAN LYHENNYS". If Vero asks, this trail is your shield against gift tax.
3. **Equal or Separate Loans?** Are you lending to them as a couple, or are you lending to your daughter who is then buying a flat with her boyfriend? The contract template above assumes you are lending to them *together as a couple*. If you only want to lend to your daughter, the contract should only be with her, and she will own a larger share of the flat.
